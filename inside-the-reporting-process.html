Inside the reporting process

INSIDE TRACK EDITORIAL DESK · SAMPLE ARTICLE

A strong report begins with a question, not a conclusion. Journalists identify a public-interest issue, map what is already known and establish what evidence could confirm or challenge the initial lead. They seek documents, interview relevant sources, corroborate accounts and give subjects a fair opportunity to respond. Editors review accuracy, context, legal exposure and clarity before publication. Good reporting also preserves its working trail: notes, source records, documents and corrections. This sample explainer is a framework; specific case studies should be linked to original, verifiable reporting.
